## Workout Types

### Easy Run
- Most runs should be easy — no faster than 7:45/mi
- Running with friends is encouraged; don't stress about pace, it's the miles we're after
- If a run IS faster than 7:45 (friends sped up, etc.), reduce other runs to compensate using weighted mileage rules (see below)

### Threshold Development
A Sub Three-specific workout combining ideas from several coaches. The goal is to practice running slightly faster than lactate threshold, training the body to clear lactate more efficiently.

- **Pace:** 6:30/mi — faster than the 6:51 target marathon pace, just above threshold
- **Structure:** At least 1 mile warmup, threshold miles in the middle, at least 1 mile cooldown
- **Distance:** Start at 5 miles total, build up to ~8 miles max (6:30 is hard to sustain beyond that)
- **Frequency:** 1-2x per week as the runner is up for it
- **Key rule:** If not feeling well, back off and replace with an easy run. Miles are always more important than threshold practice.

### Yasso 800s (Modified)
A version of the famous Yasso workout, also targeting lactate clearance and threshold improvement. Fun track workout.

- **Structure:** Warmup → 800m at 3:00 → 400-800m jog recovery → repeat → cooldown
- **Adjustment:** Scale the number of repeats to fit the day's target mileage (full Yasso protocol is quite long; we just adjust to fit)

### Long Runs
- Long easy runs — no faster than 7:45/mi
- Miles are what we want; breaks are OK, but run as continuously as possible
- Great time to practice fuel and hydration logistics (carrying gels, drinking, etc.)
- Nutrition strategy covered separately

### Marathon Race Pace (Final Race Prep + Taper)
The "hidden workout" — the mileage drops but the long run becomes a race pace rehearsal.

- **Distance:** 18-20 miles total, including warmup and cooldown
- **Structure:** Start easy, build so that the **last few miles are at race pace (6:51/mi)**, then at least 1 mile cooldown
- **Purpose:** Feel what race pace is like when fatigued. This is not about running the whole thing at pace — it's about finishing at pace.
- **Key:** It's critical to be able to hold race pace at the end, especially as fatigue sets in. This builds confidence and pacing instinct.

### Fartlek & Trail Runs
- Recommend whenever they fit for the runner — variety and fun matter
- Trail runs are great for strength and mental reset
- Catch: may need to scale back other runs based on weighted mileage (harder effort = more "load" than easy miles)

## Entry Point Rules

- **New to running / low mileage:** Start at Phase 0. Can take as long as needed.
- **≥25 miles/week:** Start at Phase 1.
- **30-35 miles/week:** Jump into Phase 1 at the week matching their volume.
- **Experienced, recent marathons, 30-40 miles/week:** Can shorten Phase 2 (3 on, 1 rest instead of 5 on, 1 rest).
- **Very experienced, close to goal recently:** Can reduce length of early phases.

## Early Workout Options (Phase 1-2)
For runners who "need speed" and can't believe slow running makes you faster:
- **Threshold Development:** 1mi warmup + middle at 6:30/mi pace + 1mi cooldown
- **Short Yasso 800s:** 800m at ~3:00 with 1-2 lap recovery, total ≤5 miles

These are concessions — the physiology doesn't require them yet, but some runners need the psychological boost. Replace 1-2 easy runs per week max.

## Key Principles
- Phase 0 and Phase 1 are about rhythm and habit, not fitness
- Rest weeks drop the long run, keep base runs
- 6-week phases (5+1) shift to 4-week phases (3+1) at Phase 5
- Rest week mileage rises as overall volume rises (33 → 36 → 48 → 50)
- Peak mileage: 68 miles/week (Phase 9)
- Longest single run: 24 miles (Phase 10)
- Final Race Prep replaces volume with race pace quality
- Taper maintains some race pace touches while dropping volume

## Living Plan — The Two-Way Feedback Loop

The plan is not a static document. It changes in two directions:

### Coach-Initiated Adjustments
The AI continuously assesses how the runner is progressing and may recommend changes:
- **Extend a phase** — runner needs more time at a mileage level before stepping up
- **Illness / injury / life** — runner missed time, plan needs to absorb the gap
- **Exceptional progress** — runner is ahead of schedule, can compress or skip ahead
- **Target race day pressure** — if there's a fixed race date, adjustments get more complex (compress phases vs. accept lower probability vs. suggest a different race)

### Runner-Requested Changes
The runner can ask for modifications at any time:
- Move the long run to a different day
- Swap workout types
- Shorten the overall plan
- Skip a phase
- Add more rest

**The key principle:** We WILL help the runner make any change they ask for. But the probability and injury risk numbers update immediately to reflect reality. The runner always sees the consequences of their choices clearly. We don't gatekeep — we inform.

This is the core product differentiator: a living plan with real-time feedback on what every decision means for their goal.
